## 1. *Skill Depth in Tabletop Board Games* — §II, §III, §VI, §VII-B — now `[V]` in full

Completes the partial read of 2026-08-24. **R-18 was proposed on §VII-A alone; §VI settles it.**

### 1.1 R-18 CONFIRMED — the miscalibration is per-game and heterogeneous

Verbatim, §VI:

> *"Optimal settings may vary both by player count and the computational budget. NTBEA is used
> for parameter optimisation. This gives a tuned set of parameter settings for each budget and
> player count. **For some games it was found that the same agent was robustly best across all
> player counts and budgets, while for others this varied.**"*

That is the sentence R-18 needed. The miscalibration is **not uniform** — it is heterogeneous
across games, which is precisely the condition that turns a limitation into a **confound in
`β₁`**. `SURFACES.md` puts all four thresholds on the ladder and §5.2 compares them across
games.

**G-20 is therefore a budget question, not a framing question.** Their tuning is done with
**NTBEA** over the Table I space — a per-game optimisation run that `BUDGET.md` does not
cost at all.

### 1.2 The algorithmic space is larger than "MCTS with a rollout budget"

Table I — the space they tune over:

| Parameter | Values | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `K` | 0.01, 1.0, 100 | 1.0 |
| `ε` | 0.03, 0.1, 0.3 | — |
| Selection | UCT, UCB-Tuned, Regret Matching | UCB |
| Rollout policy | Random, MAST | Random |
| MAST `γ` | 0.0, 0.5 | — |
| **Rollout length** | 0, 3, 10, 30, 100, 300, 1000 | 1000 |
| Tree type | Standard, MultiTree, SelfOnly | Standard |
| Heuristic | WinOnly, WinPlus, Ordinal, Score, ScorePlus, **Leader** | WinOnly |

Also §VI: their default is **Open Loop Information Set MCTS with the state redeterminised at
the root each iteration** — *"because most of the games have imperfect information"* — not
vanilla MCTS. Theory §1.2 says only *"MCTS with rollout budgets `B_j = B_0 · 2^j`"*. For
imperfect-information tabletop games that under-specifies the agent.

### 1.3 A compute reference point — and it is reassuring

Their run, from §VI: budgets `x ∈ {8,16,32,64,128,256,512}ms`, **28 tournaments** (full pairwise
grid, not just 2x-vs-x), **1000 games per pairing**, positions rotated, same seed set, **run
twice** (Tuned and Classic), over 16 games and multiple player counts.

Order **10⁵–10⁶ matches on TAG**, by one PhD student. **This is external evidence that the
~10⁶-match figure in `BUDGET.md` §1 is achievable on this framework** — which R-17 puts under
contest from the other direction. It does not resolve R-17; `M ≈ 785` is a different question
from whether 10⁶ matches is runnable. But the feasibility half of the budget now has a
comparator, and it is the only one this project has ever had. **Asserted, not verified** — it
is inferred from their described design, not from a stated runtime.

### 1.4 Their estimator needed regularisation, in exactly our regime

§VI: *"L2 regularisation (λ = 0.0001) was used on `M`, `r` and `log β` to impose a weak prior
of low skill and a flat skill ladder. **This was found to be necessary as otherwise the high
noise in low-skill games could cause overfitting to the parameters.**"*

Theory §5.3 plans to *"fit the parametric curve `W(p) = Φ(ρ(p,L))` by maximum likelihood and
invert analytically"*, unregularised. **`chaos-games` drives games into the high-noise,
low-skill-expression regime deliberately, by sweeping `p`** — and the threshold lives near the
top of that sweep. Their experience says the fit destabilises exactly there.

**The collapse-threshold estimator is on `SURFACES.md`.** This is prior evidence about how it
will misbehave, from the same framework, and it should reach the build memo when one is written.

### 1.5 An argumentative asset — their headline parameter is not identifiable; ours is bounded

§VII-B, verbatim:

> *"`M` in Model 1 was defined to be the adjusted win rate achievable asymptotically as the
> computational budget increases. Hence values above 1.0 are nonsensical, and yet Table III has
> several examples where this is the case. These are twinned with low `r` values... the model
> fit only gets anywhere near `M` well beyond 1024ms and hence the range of the data. **In these
> cases `M` is not usefully interpretable.**"*

**Budget is unbounded, so their asymptote escapes their data.** `p ∈ [0,1]` is bounded, and
`G_1` is a defined endpoint the theory document already anchors (§2.1: *"`G_1` is a Markov chain
with no agency"*). A `p`-sweep can cover its **entire** domain; a budget ladder cannot.

That is a real, defensible reason the injected-noise instrument adds something the budget ladder
cannot give — **better conditioning, not just a different question** — and it is the strongest
material yet for **G-19**. It is also a `[V]` fact about the incumbent's own paper, so it can
carry weight.

## 2. *Evaluation of PI-MCTS in Imperfect-Information Games* — CoG 2026 — `[V-sub]`

Ravichandran, Goodman & Lucas. Abstract, §I and §VII read in the original.

**R-14 narrows again — `Δ_model` survives.** This is an **algorithm comparison**, not a
formalisation: PI-MCTS vs OL-MCTS vs IS-MCTS across **twelve TAG games** with three aggregation
policies. *"PI-MCTS matches or exceeds OL-MCTS but rarely outperforms IS-MCTS."* They
**formalise no model-mismatch quantity, inject no noise, and use no skill ladder as an
instrument.** They name strategy fusion and non-locality as the known theoretical issues and
cite the literature for them; they do not measure a mismatch gap.

So `Δ_model` — the noise-aware minus noise-blind threshold gap — is **still unoccupied**, and
§5.4 may keep it. It is a **related-work obligation**, which is what R-14 has been since
2026-08-22.

**But it widens R-18.** Verbatim: *"Performance strongly depends on the determinization count
and varies by game"*, and in results: *"in four other games (Tic-Tac-Toe, Monopoly Deal,
Dominion & Wonders7), PI-MCTS performed worse than the baseline"*, while in **Exploding
Kittens** *"high stochasticity and low skill expression resulted in no significant performance
gaps."*

Two things follow. First, the **information-handling algorithm** is a second per-game axis on
top of §1.1's parameter axis. Second — and this one is directly on this project's target —
**there already exists a named TAG game where high stochasticity leaves no measurable skill
gap.** That is `τ_skill ≈ 0` observed in passing, in print, by the incumbent, without anyone
setting out to measure it.

*Also worth noting: they use the phrase **"skill expression"** — the term ledger G-5 proposes
retitling around. It is theirs too.*

## 3. *Following the Leader in Multiplayer Tabletop Games* — FDG 2023 — `[V-sub]`

**My title-based inference was wrong, and this is the correction.** `LITSEARCH-2026-08-24.md`
§1.1 lists this as bearing on **§3.4's feedback coefficient `θ`** — snowballing and leader
dynamics. It does not. That entry was `[T]`, from a publication list, and it was a guess.

What it actually is: a study of **agent objective functions** in multiplayer games — binary win
rate vs ordinal rank vs raw score — and of **rollout length**. *"We find that the games
considered largely fall into two groups. In one it is helpful to focus just on one's own score
during the game, and then shift to beating opponents only in the end-game. In the other, larger,
group it is better to 'Follow the Leader' and constantly track one's relative position."*

**Nothing here is about a game's feedback structure.** "Following the leader" is what the
*agent* does, not what the *game* does to a lead. §3.4's `θ` — whether early leads compound or
decay — is untouched by it, and remains unoccupied by anything retrieved so far.

**It is the third R-18 axis.** It is the source of the `Leader` heuristic in §1.2's Table I, and:
*"Games in which long-term planning is required, or when the full score is only known at the end
of the game benefit from a full rollout, while games with adversarial counter-moves benefit from
a short rollout length."* Per-game again.

**It is also multiplayer throughout**, which is ledger G-8 territory — this project restricts to
two players, and the incumbent's tooling and objectives are built for `n > 2`.
