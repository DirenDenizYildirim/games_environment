## 2. The companion — Goodman, Perez-Liebana & Lucas, CoG 2024 `[V]`

*"Measuring Randomness in Tabletop Games,"* Game AI Research Group, QMUL. IEEE CoG 2024.

**It contains the length analysis the ToG paper lacks.** Figure 2, caption verbatim:

> "Plots of random seed effects as **the number of rounds to win in Love Letter is
> increased**. For 1 round the shuffle often means one player is predetermined to win. As
> more rounds are required the effect reduces... The thick red line above the x-axis marks
> the Trimmed Span in each case. **This is 1.0 for 1 round at the top, with its lowest value
> of 0.64 at the bottom for 50 rounds.**"

**Our claim survives, narrowed.** What they did is one game, varying a **rule parameter**
(rounds to win), measuring the **seed effect on outcome spread** — not a skill-collapse
threshold, not across games, and **with no functional form fitted**. `L^(−1/2)` and the
cross-game regression from structure are not taken.

**But the qualitative relation is now published**, at CoG, by the incumbent, on TAG. No
sentence may present *"randomness's effect depends on match length"* as this project's
observation. What is left to claim is the **threshold**, the **derived exponent**, and the
**prediction from structure**.

**A quantitative tension worth pricing early.** Trimmed Span 1.0 → 0.64 across a **50×**
increase in length is far shallower than `L^(−1/2)` implies (~0.14). Most likely these are
different quantities — spread of outcome vs. location of a collapse threshold — and the
theory document should say which, explicitly, rather than leave a reader to notice the
numbers do not sit together. If they are *not* different quantities, §3's functional form has
a problem.

### Two things in this paper that are not in `RISKS.md`

**(a) Their declared future work is this project's question.** §VI, verbatim:

> "**Variation in ability.** The agents used have deliberately been identical in terms of
> ability. It will be interesting if the same patterns occur for different skill levels both
> for homogeneous agents (**does greater skill increase or decrease the ability to mitigate
> stochasticity**) and heterogeneous... **Are there games for which randomness has a large
> effect on beginner players, but not for more skilled players?**"

That is §6.8 and a large part of Paper A, written as declared future work in 2024 — by the
group that owns the framework, at the target venue. The ToG 2025 paper delivers part of it.
New risk **R-16**.

**(b) The compute budget rests on a model they have publicly called invalid.** §VI, verbatim:

> "if each individual game played has an actual p that varies widely around a mean p′ due to
> the random seed then this means **more games need to be run for the same confidence
> interval** on the result. **Calculating the correction required is current work in
> progress.**"

Ledger **G-11** sets δ = 0.05 → M ≈ 785 matches per condition, and `BUDGET.md` §1 derives the
whole compute figure from it — through a **binomial model with fixed `p`**. The incumbent
states that model is inadequate under exactly the condition this project studies, and is
actively producing the correction. Both the statistics and the budget are exposed, and the
correction may publish before submission. New risk **R-17**.

## 3. The ToG paper — *Seeding for Success* `[T]`

Goodman, Perez-Liebana & Lucas, IEEE ToG 17(3):777–786 (2025), arXiv:2503.02686. **Exact
title: "Seeding for Success: Skill and Stochasticity in Tabletop Games."**

Quantities: Entropy of the win-rate distribution (2% buckets), Span, **Trimmed Span** (central
95%), and an Outliers count against a 99% binomial CI. Skill ladder = MCTS thinking time
**2ms / 10ms / 50ms / 250ms plus a random agent**. Randomness is varied by **fixing game
seeds — not by injecting noise**. No threshold at which skill stops separating is defined. No
length relation in this paper.

**R-15 confirmed as a real opposition**, reported verbatim as: "There is a clear pattern in
all or most games for the effect of the random seed to increase as the skill of the players
increases."

**Two deltas this hands us**, both usable: their instrument is **seed variation**, ours is
**noise injection** — different operators; and their skill ladder is the same mechanism as
**G-3**, which is corroboration that budget-doubling is acceptable at this venue.

**Tier note.** `[T]` — fetch-layer summary only. **May not carry a novelty claim**
(`SEARCH-PROTOCOL.md` §4). The `pdftotext` route in §1 would upgrade it in ~20 minutes and it
is the nearest work in the field, so this should not stay at `[T]`.

## 4. R-13 — Machado et al., sticky actions `[T]`

*"Revisiting the Arcade Learning Environment,"* JAIR 2018, arXiv:1709.06009. §5.2: at step `t`
the executed action is the intended `a` with probability `1−ς` and the previous action
`a_{t−1}` with probability `ς`; benchmark value **ς = 0.25**.

Purpose is stated as distinguishing "brittle, open-loop solutions" from "robust, closed-loop
solutions" — a robustness protocol. **They never sweep `ς`** (only 0.0 and 0.25), and connect
it to **neither skill separation nor episode length**.

**The delta is clean and defensible:** action-**repeat** at a fixed value for robustness
testing, versus uniform **replacement** swept across `p` to locate a threshold and predict it
from length. Contribution (1) needs rewording, not dropping. `[T]`, so the sentence itself
still needs the original.

## 5. R-14 — Long, Sturtevant, Buro & Furtak `[V]`

*"Understanding the Success of Perfect Information Monte Carlo Sampling in Game Tree
Search,"* AAAI 2010, 24(1):134–140.

**Strategy fusion** — PIMC "(incorrectly) believes it can use a different strategy in each
world," where an information set spans multiple perfect-information scenarios and one strategy
must serve all. **Non-locality** — in an imperfect-information game "a node's value may depend
on other regions of the game tree not contained within its subtree," because the opponent
steers play using private information.

Three measurable tree properties: **Leaf Correlation `lc`**, **Bias `b`**, **Disambiguation
factor `df`**, each in [0,1] and estimable in real games by random playouts.

**No model-mismatch quantity is formalised anywhere.** A grep for `model mismatch|mismatch|
wrong model|incorrect model|imperfect model|model error` returns one unrelated hit. They
measure PIMC's **average loss against a Nash-equilibrium (CFR) player** as a function of tree
properties. **No noise injection. No skill/budget ladder. Tree depth held constant at 8** —
length is controlled out, not studied.

**Finding — R-14 is narrower than "the largest unpatched hole," and partly an asset.** Their
mismatch is **informational** (searching a perfect-information determinization of an
imperfect-information game); Δ_model is **execution-noise** (an agent that models the noise
process vs. one that does not). Different object.

More useful: they establish the precedent that **structural game properties predict when an
algorithmic mismatch causes loss** — which is the *shape* of this project's claim. Cited
properly, they are a **supporting** citation for the form of the argument, not only a
competitor. §8.2 must cite them; Δ_model need not drop to a measurement. **G-14 has a third
option K1 did not list.**
